## What this suggests (hypotheses, not conclusions)

1. **Repository-aware ticket generation exists**, in agents, extensions and open skills. "Reads your code" alone is not a differentiator.
2. **None of the sources describes per-claim status with inspectable citations**, or an explicit separation of verified facts from inferences and product decisions. That is the candidate differentiator (H3 in [`business-hypotheses.md`](business-hypotheses.md)). Absence in marketing copy is not proof of absence in the product; check by trying them.
3. **Audit of existing tickets** is covered at the "fix unclear tickets" level by marketplace apps, without code grounding in what was found (H9).
4. **The platforms are moving toward the same space.** Atlassian, GitHub and Linear are all adding agents that read Jira or Linear plus code. Their reach is the main strategic risk (H6).
5. One commentary found while searching argues AI-written tickets often contain only the solution and omit problem definition, constraints and decision context. Treat as a lead for the audit rubric, not as evidence.

## Gaps to close before using this in a pitch

- Try Rovo work item creation, Rovo Dev, Linear Agent and Scrummy on the fixture repositories and score the outputs with the same rubric ([`evaluation-rubric.md`](evaluation-rubric.md)).
- Pricing and plan limits for each product.
- Whether Rovo's creation flow uses repository context when GitHub is connected.
- Whether any product marks unverified claims or asks for product decisions explicitly.
- Search beyond what was covered: Jira Product Discovery, Productboard, Notion AI, Zenhub, Shortcut, and requirements-focused startups.
